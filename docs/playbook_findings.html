Distilling the operator's edge

We taught a machine to see the four playbooks the way he trades them — then found out where his edge actually lives, and it wasn't where we expected.

Window Jul 3 – Jul 11, 2026 Gates P0–P3 closed · Flywheel A–B run Universe IWM / SPY · 5yr option-path

The live book trades one of his four playbooks. Flash, exhaustion, and range — his real bread and butter — had no strategies in the loop at all. So instead of inventing strategies and hoping they matched him, we inverted it: start from four years of his own fills, learn what he does, teach a detector to see it, and test whether the mechanical version makes money.

FINDINGSWhat we learned, gate by gate

P0 · His playbooks, made precise

Prose became taggable rules, corrected by him directly. The corrections mattered: exhaustion is a symbol at its ~85–90th percentile stretch, any symbol, any timeframe; trend anchors on the 10-VMA, not VWAP; range includes earnings-gap continuation; flash is explicitly counter-trend.

P1 · He is a reversal trader

1,443 timestamped fills → 390 trade episodes, labeled by playbook and corrected across two phone reviews until the machine matched his eye 94% on confident calls. The distribution was the surprise:

PlaybookEpisodesRead
Flash reversal142his base mode
Exhaustion reversal117his second instrument
Trend continuation51the one already live
None of the four~80"bored / Fed-event" — where losses cluster

The machine first read him as trend-heavy; his corrections revealed reversals dominate — and the losing trades are the ones fitting no playbook, echoing his own written rule: "not worth it if you cannot follow rules."

P2 · Two of three playbooks mechanize cleanly

Can a rule fire where he fired without firing all day? Swept every 5 minutes of a year:

  • Flash — yes. A ≥0.15-ATR flush into a fresh extreme fires ~7× more at his entries than at random, ~4 alerts/day.
  • Trend — yes. Established trend + an active stalling pullback: ~9× selectivity.
  • Exhaustion — not yet (honest fork). The context is easy; his timing varies too much across 5 clean examples to pin. Logged as "eye not captured," never "no edge."

P3 · The edge is real, thin, and selection is the missing layer

Five years, ~48k fires, traded as real options with his native exit profiles, IV-banded, cost-haircut, three regimes:

  • Gross edge is real and broad — positive in 44 of 48 regime cells (+0.5 to +3.4%/trade before costs).
  • Taking every fire loses money — ~12 fires/day, he takes 1–2; the thin edge dies under costs.
  • Best-of-day doubled the edge — but that's hindsight. Every live-executable filter gave most of it back.
The named finding · the selection gap

There is a 2–4%-per-trade gap between what the mechanical setups earn and what his real trading earns — and that gap is his discretionary choice of which fires to take. We measured the thing that was always folklore.

THE TURNThen Phase B changed our minds

We started the Flywheel believing: learn which fires he takes → filter the detector → mechanical profit. The first two arrows held. The last one broke.

  • We CAN predict his clicks. He takes early-session fires (earliest quartile 8%, latest 1%). Out-of-sample a scorer separates his takes (8.6%) from his skips (0.3%).
  • But predicting his clicks doesn't make money. Taking his high-score fires scored worse on the option path, not better:

What it means

His edge on the fast plays is not in which setup he picks — it's in the parts the backtest strips out: entry execution and live exit management. He legs in better than mid; his real hold is a 28-minute scalp while the mechanical profile runs an R-ladder out to 90 minutes; and early option-buying faces an IV/theta headwind a hold can't beat but a fast scalp does. Trend is the one that helped mechanically (+1.0%) — the slowest, least execution-sensitive play, and the one already live. That's the tell.

The negative result is worth more than a marginal pass: it stops us shipping an autonomous flash/exhaustion lane that would have quietly lost money, and it redirects the build toward measuring the human layer. The yardstick was right — option-path, cost-aware, out-of-sample, lookahead-audited. No repeat of the old mis-kill.

THE PLANHow we leverage it — the Flywheel

Setup, selection, and execution are one P&L stack; we run all three at once, wired so each feeds the next. The B finding shifted where the effort goes.

Two roads out — the evidence favors the first

  • Consultation is the product. The detectors are a strong candidate surface (flash coverage 93%); the human supplies the alpha the backtest can't. The research wins by making his discretionary trading faster and better-armed — measured, not replaced.
  • A mechanical lane still ships IF Phase C recalibrates a profile — e.g. a fast-scalp FLASH exit matched to his real 28-minute hold — that clears the bar out-of-sample. Trend's +1.0% says the slower plays mechanize first, carrying their native exit profile into a shadow lane. That closes the exact gap that started this program.
His setups are real and mechanizable — but his 2–4%/trade edge over them is execution and fast exits a fixed-profile backtest can't see. So the payoff is a consultation tool that arms his discretion, plus a measurement of his execution alpha that may recalibrate the exit profiles — slow plays first.
